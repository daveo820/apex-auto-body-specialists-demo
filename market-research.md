### Patterns the winners share
1. **Phone and estimate in the header.** UltraColor, Caliber and Gerber all keep a tap to call number and an estimate button visible on every page.
2. **Photo first estimates.** "Virtual Estimate" (Michael Jordan), "Online Collision Estimate" (Gerber), "Start your estimate" (Caliber). Customers expect to send pictures before they drive over.
3. **Insurance explained.** Every top site has an insurance page or FAQ. The chains sell their insurer partnerships; independents sell the customer's right to choose.
4. **Reviews near the top**, usually Google stars plus three quotes.
5. **Services as a grid of cards**, one line each, linking to detail.
6. **Hours and map** on the home page for location searches.
7. **What they get wrong:** heavy pages (5 to 20 MB), slow LCP on phones (8 to 27 s), stock photos of cars that are not theirs.

## 4. The opening
Every chain and most dealers in the area sell their insurer partnerships. Apex Auto Body Specialists' own old site sold the opposite: "Work for you (the insured or claimant), not the Insurance Company." North Carolina law backs that up (G.S. 58-3-180: the claimant selects the repair shop; the insurer pays the same either way). None of the seven home pages above leads with the customer's right to choose (Michael Jordan has an Insurance page; the chains lead with their own process). That is the angle, and it is the shop's own words, not ours.

## 5. Pitch points
1. Google's "Website" button on a 152 review listing goes to a domain that does not exist. Every tap is a lost lead.
2. The demo weighs a fraction of the competition (the top seven average about 6 MB on phones) and loads its hero with no photos to wait for.
3. A "your rights" page quoting the actual NC statute, on brand with the shop's own 2018 message.
4. A photo first estimate form, the pattern the top sites use (shown as a demo; nothing is sent).
5. Reviews with dates, word for word.

## 6. Risks and caveats
- The independent, non contract stance comes from the 2018 archived site. Confirm Darin still runs that way before saying it to anyone.
- Five Google reviews were all posted on 20 Feb 2026, read like templates and name no car or person. We did not quote them. If they were solicited or bought, that is a risk to the listing; we should not mention it in a pitch, but Davis should know.
- Hours differ: Google says 9 to 5; the 2018 site said 8:30 to 5. We used Google.
- allbiz says "locations in Cary, Raleigh, Holly Springs" and "approval from all major insurers". Not verified; not used.
- SOS status is from a search snippet, not the SOS page itself.
- Competitor scores are one Lighthouse run each from the box; they move from run to run. Use them as a range, not a promise.
- We never claim the demo will rank or bring in calls. We show process: speed, clarity, the right to choose page, photo first estimates.
